## Abstract
This is the first study to assess science’s major discoveries that cover all nobel-prize and major non-nobel-prize discoveries throughout history. We find that after designing powerful tools—from X-ray methods and the electron microscope to spectrometers and chromatography—we have sparked dozens of major breakthroughs that were impossible without them. We also uncover that most discoveries since 1975 are triggered within 4 years of creating the needed tool, and many are triggered simultaneously. Building on the evidence of science’s major discoveries, we develop a general _new methods-driven discovery theory_.

## Introduction
Surprisingly, in assessing science’s over 750 major discoveries, we find that they consistently share one common pattern: across fields and time periods, discoverers rely on a new method or tool that triggers their new breakthrough finding—commonly made soon after developing them.
